Fish care

Betta Care Guide

Bettas are hardy fish, but that does not mean they thrive in tiny containers. A healthy Betta splendens needs warm, stable water, gentle filtration, room to explore and an environment that lets it behave like a fish rather than simply survive.

Beginner to intermediate Freshwater fish Betta splendens

The basics

Warm, clean and calm beats small and decorative.

Bettas come from tropical environments and need warm water. They also prefer relatively gentle flow because many domestic varieties have long fins that make swimming against strong current tiring.

Temperature 24–28°C
pH About 6.5–7.8
Ammonia 0 ppm
Nitrite 0 ppm
Flow Gentle
A labyrinth organ is not an excuse for poor water. Bettas can breathe atmospheric air, but they still depend on clean, heated aquarium water and proper biological filtration.

Aquarium size

Give the fish enough room to use the tank.

Very small containers are difficult to keep stable and give the fish little room for natural movement. A larger aquarium is easier to heat, filter and maintain, and it gives you space for plants, resting areas and enrichment.

Shape matters as well. Bettas regularly visit the surface, so an aquarium with reasonable horizontal swimming space and easy surface access is more useful than a very tall, narrow container.

Cups and unheated bowls are not suitable permanent homes. They are difficult to keep stable and do not provide the space, heating or filtration needed for long-term care.

Heating & filtration

Stable temperature and gentle water movement.

Heating

Use a reliable aquarium heater if room temperature cannot keep the tank consistently within the suitable range. Sudden temperature swings are best avoided.

Filtration

Sponge filters and adjustable low-flow filters work well. Strong current can exhaust long-finned fish and make the aquarium less comfortable to use.

Surface access

Bettas regularly take air from the surface. Make sure the fish can reach it easily and that floating plants do not completely block access.

Lid

Bettas can jump. A secure lid reduces the risk while also helping maintain warm, humid air above the water surface.

Plants & enrichment

A bare tank wastes half the opportunity.

Bettas are curious fish. Plants, wood, broad leaves and gentle shaded areas give them places to explore and rest. Live plants can also improve the feel of the aquarium and give the fish more visual cover.

  • Broad leaves can act as resting spots near the surface.
  • Floating plants can create shade, but leave open surface access.
  • Avoid sharp plastic decorations that can tear delicate fins.
  • Change or rearrange small parts of the environment occasionally for enrichment.

Feeding

Bettas are carnivorous and easy to overfeed.

A good diet can include quality betta pellets along with suitable frozen or live foods. Variety is useful, but portion control matters just as much as food quality.

  • Feed small portions rather than one oversized meal.
  • Remove uneaten food if it begins to break down.
  • Use frozen or live foods as part of a varied diet, not as an excuse to overfeed.
  • Do not rely on generic flakes alone if better species-appropriate options are available.
A constantly begging betta is not necessarily hungry. Many bettas quickly learn that people near the tank can mean food. Feeding every time the fish asks is a very efficient way to create digestive and water-quality problems.

Behaviour

Individual temperament matters.

Some bettas are bold and interactive. Others are calmer or more territorial. Behaviour can change with age, environment and the presence of other animals.

Watch the fish rather than assuming every betta will behave the same way. Persistent glass surfing, clamped fins, lethargy or refusing food can be signs that something in the environment needs attention.

Tank mates

Compatibility is never guaranteed.

Bettas can sometimes live with suitable community fish or invertebrates, but temperament varies. The tank must be large enough, heavily structured and stocked with species that do not harass, nip or outcompete the betta.

Long-finned, brightly coloured or fin-nipping fish are generally poor choices. Shrimp may be ignored by one betta and hunted by another.

Have a backup plan. If you try a community setup, be prepared to separate animals if chasing, fin damage or constant stress appears.

Male & female bettas

Do not treat them as automatic tank mates.

Adult males are territorial and should not be housed together. Males and females should also not simply be placed together as a permanent pair. Breeding introductions are deliberate, supervised setups and are not the same thing as normal community housing.

Female groups are sometimes attempted, but so-called sororities can be unstable and are not a beginner setup.

Water changes

Routine maintenance keeps problems boring.

Regular partial water changes help control nitrate and dissolved waste. Clean the aquarium sensibly without sterilising the filter or replacing all biological media at once.

Match replacement water reasonably closely in temperature and always use a suitable dechlorinator when treating tap water.

Common mistakes

The usual ways betta setups go wrong.

  • Keeping the fish in an unheated bowl or tiny container.
  • Using a filter with current strong enough to push the fish around.
  • Overfeeding because the betta always appears interested in food.
  • Using sharp decorations that damage fins.
  • Adding tank mates without enough space or a separation plan.
  • Assuming surface breathing means filtration or oxygenation does not matter.
  • Ignoring a secure lid because the fish “has never jumped before”.
